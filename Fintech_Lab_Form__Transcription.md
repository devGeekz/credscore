**9. What problem does your fintech idea or concept aim to solve?**


*Maximum 100 words. **

> Buying and selling between strangers in Ghana's informal market is risky for both sides. Buyers pay upfront and may never receive goods; sellers ship goods and may never get paid. Cash and plain mobile-money transfers offer no protection — no hold, no proof, and no recourse when a deal goes wrong. Trust depends on knowing the person personally, which blocks trade between strangers and limits small businesses from growing beyond their immediate community.
> 

**10. What is your fintech idea or concept, and how does it address this problem?**


*Maximum 100 words. **

> SikaLock is an escrow service built on USSD and mobile money. A buyer and seller agree a deal by phone; the buyer dials *384# and the agreed amount is held in escrow — not sent to the seller. The seller ships, the buyer confirms delivery, and funds are released instantly. Either side can raise a dispute, freezing payment for review, with automatic deadlines (7 days to ship, 14 days to resolve) so money never waits forever. No app, no bank account, no internet needed.
> 

**11. Who will benefit most from your idea?**


*Maximum 50 words. **

> Ghana's informal market traders and their customers — market sellers, artisans, riders, and small online shops — plus the growing number of Ghanaians who buy from strangers online but only trust cash or known contacts.
> 

**12. What makes your idea unique?**


*Tell us what is new, different or better about your approach. Maximum 75 words. **

> Escrow usually requires an app, a bank, or a tech-savvy user. SikaLock runs entirely on USSD — the same *384# menu every feature phone already uses — wired directly to MTN MoMo and Africa's Talking SMS, so both parties get real-time SMS receipts at every step. It works on a Kente seller's basic phone, needs no app download, and enforces deadlines automatically so funds can't be frozen indefinitely.
>
